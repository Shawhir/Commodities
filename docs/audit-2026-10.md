# Dashboard audit, October 2026

What each part of the dashboard is for, whether the evidence says it helps, and what is still
missing for a view on gold over the next quarter, 6 months, year and 5 years.

## How each measure was tested

For every measure the page uses, a walk-forward test from 2000: each month, fit a simple
line between the measure and gold's return over the next 3, 6, 12 or 60 months, using only
months whose outcome was already known, then forecast. The forecast is scored against the plain
assumption "gold does what it has done on average so far" (out-of-sample R squared: above zero
means the measure helped, below zero means it made the forecast worse). Measures that looked
useful were re-checked period by period (2000-07, 2008-15, 2016-21, 2022-26).

Script: run against the stored data; results reproduced in the tables below.

**Summary:** the only measures that helped in most periods are the two trend measures behind
your rules, and only a little. Everything else either adds nothing or works in some periods
and fails in others. That matches the research: gold's direction is very hard to forecast,
trend persistence is the most consistent small edge, and the big macro links (real yields,
the dollar) explain moves as they happen rather than ahead of time.

## What else is needed, by horizon

The honest starting point: nobody has a reliable forecast of gold's direction over any of these
horizons. What can be forecast is **how much gold is likely to move** (volatility persists and is
priced in options), **a slight tendency for trends to continue**, and over long horizons, **a
drag when gold is very expensive**. The additions below follow from that.

### Next quarter
- **Expected move from options (missing, high value).** The gold volatility index (CBOE GVZ) is
  already downloaded but not shown. It gives the range the market expects (about one in three
  quarters falls outside it). This is the one forward-looking number with a solid record.
- **Event calendar** (have): FOMC, US CPI, jobs, positioning, central bank figures.
- **Flows as they happen** (have): gold fund holdings, speculators' positioning, explained not
  predicted.

### Next 6 months
- **Trend** (have, in the rules): the most consistent small edge.
- **Where markets expect US rates to go (missing).** The 2-year Treasury yield (FRED DGS2) moves
  with expected Fed policy. It explains gold's swings better than the Fed's current rate.
- **Recession risk (missing).** The yield curve (10-year minus 3-month, FRED T10Y3M); recessions
  tend to bring rate cuts, which gold has liked.

### Next year
- **Trend and regime** (have).
- **Real yields, used as a regime question rather than a forecast** (have): has the post-2022
  pattern, where central bank buying outweighs high real yields, continued or broken?
- **Scenarios rather than a single forecast (missing).** For example: rate cuts with a weaker
  dollar, sticky inflation, or recession with a stronger dollar. Show a price range for each,
  based on how gold behaved in similar past episodes.

### Next 5 years
- **Valuation (missing, high value).** Gold against US consumer prices is at its highest ever,
  about 50-65% above the 1980 and 2011 peaks in real terms. Both earlier peaks were followed by
  long weak stretches. With only two episodes it cannot be tested, but it belongs on the page.
  Related measures: gold against the money supply (FRED M2SL) and against US federal debt
  (FRED GFDEBTN).
- **Structural demand** (partly have): the central bank buying trend, reserve shares, sanctions
  and reserve-diversification motives.
- **Supply (missing).** Mine output is roughly flat around 3,600 tonnes; recycling rises with the
  price; miners' all-in costs (well below today's price) mark a rough long-run floor. Annual
  figures, typed in from the World Gold Council.
- **US fiscal path (missing).** Deficits and debt as a share of GDP (FRED), the main long-run
  argument for gold that is not about interest rates.

### Across all horizons
- **A forecast scorecard (missing, high value).** Record every number the page publishes (odds,
  ranges, expected moves) and score it when the outcome is known. Backtests flatter; a live
  record shows what actually works.
- **Silver (gap).** The page is built around gold. Silver depends on industrial demand (solar,
  electronics) and the gold/silver ratio; if silver matters to you, it needs its own drivers.

## Done in this round
- The odds model and the analogue finder now use the market real yield (10-year TIPS) from 2003,
  with the rough estimate only before. The estimate had real rates near 1% against the market's
  2.9%, so the odds counted them as helping gold. Now they push towards falling.
- Swiss 10-year yield (OECD via FRED) and Swiss inflation (SNB data portal) added: tiles, a
  "Swiss interest rates" pressure (10-year 0.47%, real +0.12% in August 2026: holding gold costs
  a franc saver very little), and an odds input.
- The misleading "real yield proxy" tile was removed.
